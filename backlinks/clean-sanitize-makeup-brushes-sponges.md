**Title:** PSA: brush cleaning spray on a dirty brush mostly sanitizes the makeup, not the bristles

Something I wish someone had told me years ago: spraying 70% isopropyl on a brush that still has foundation in it does a lot less than it seems. The alcohol mostly hits the outer layer of product. ## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is spraying makeup brushes with alcohol enough to clean them?

Short answer: no, not on its own. As a working makeup artist, this is the mistake I see most often, and I made it myself for years.

Alcohol sprays sanitize. They don't clean, and those are two different jobs. 70% isopropyl alcohol kills many microbes, but only on surfaces it actually reaches. When a brush is still loaded with foundation, concealer or cream blush, the spray mostly lands on that outer layer of product. The bristles underneath, especially near the ferrule where makeup packs in tightest, barely get touched.

The method that works has two steps, in this order:

1. Wash. Use a gentle soap or brush cleanser with lukewarm water. Work it through the bristles in your palm or on a textured mat until the water runs clear. Keep the brush pointed downward so water doesn't seep into the ferrule and loosen the glue. Squeeze out the water, reshape the bristles, and dry the brush flat or bristles down.

2. Sanitize. Once the brush is clean and the bristles are exposed, mist or dip it in 70% isopropyl alcohol and let it evaporate fully.

Sponges need the same approach with more patience. They hold product deep in the foam, so squeeze them under running water with soap until nothing more comes out, then sanitize. Replace them every few months anyway, since the foam breaks down.

The quick spray does earn its place between clients or between colors on the same day. It's a stopgap, not a deep clean.

For a personal kit, a sensible baseline is a weekly wash for anything you use with liquids or creams.
