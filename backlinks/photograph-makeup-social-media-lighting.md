## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does my makeup look flat in photos, and how can I fix the lighting?

This is one of the most common problems I see, and it is almost always the light, not the makeup or the camera.

Most people use a ring light because it is marketed for beauty content. A ring light shines straight at the face from the front. Direct frontal light erases shadows, and shadows are what give a face its shape. Without them, blush turns into a flat patch of color, contour disappears, and highlighter just looks shiny instead of dimensional.

I learned this the hard way. Last March I shot a bridal trial in a Brooklyn apartment around 7 p.m. I used a cream blush in soft rose and a glassy lip. In person it looked beautiful. Under my ring light, the cheek became one flat pink disc and the photo looked like a coupon.

Here is what fixes it:

1. Turn the ring light off, or at least stop using it as your main light.
2. Put the person beside a window so the light hits the face from the side. Soft daylight at roughly 45 degrees is ideal.
3. If the sun is harsh, hang a thin white curtain or sheer fabric over the glass to diffuse it.
4. Face the window slightly, so both eyes catch light, and keep the shadowed side of the face from going too dark. A white card or sheet of foam board held opposite the window bounces light back.
5. Check the photo at full size before you commit to a shoot, because small screens hide flatness.

Also adjust the makeup a little for the camera. Blend blush a bit higher and softer, and add a touch more definition than you would for real life.
