## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How does the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act (MoCRA) affect professional makeup artists?

Short answer: most of the legal obligations fall on manufacturers, but MoCRA changes how a careful makeup artist should work.

Some background. MoCRA was signed on December 29, 2022, and it's the biggest update to US cosmetics law since 1938. Before it, the FDA had no authority to order a cosmetic recall, and companies weren't required to report serious adverse events. Now the FDA is putting deadlines in place for facility registration, product listing, safety substantiation, and adverse event reporting, along with labeling requirements, including a contact for reporting problems.

I'm a professional makeup artist, and this became real for me during a bridal trial when a client's eyelid swelled up about twenty minutes after application. Before MoCRA, I'd have treated it as a one-off and moved on. Now I think about the paper trail.

Here's what I'd suggest for working artists:

1. Keep a client log. Record products used, brand, shade, and batch or lot number. If a reaction happens, you can trace it.

2. Patch test where it makes sense, especially for new clients or sensitive areas like the eyes.

3. Know where to report. Serious reactions can be reported to the brand and to the FDA. Brands now have a legal duty to track these.

4. Vet your suppliers. Favor brands with clear labeling, ingredient transparency, and a visible contact for complaints. Be cautious with products that have no traceable manufacturer.

5. Watch for decanted or repackaged products. If you're depotting or mixing, your own hygiene and labeling matter even more.

None of this requires you to become a regulatory expert. It's about treating documentation as part of professional practice, the same way we treat sanitation.
