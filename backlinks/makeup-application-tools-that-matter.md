## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What makeup application tools do you actually need, and which are a waste of money?

As a professional makeup artist, I'd say you need far fewer tools than most kits suggest. Four things do the majority of the work: a damp sponge, one dense base brush, one fluffy blending brush and a clean spoolie.

The sponge is the most important. Dampen it, squeeze out the excess water, and press (don't drag) foundation or concealer into the skin. It sheers out the product, avoids streaks and makes base look like skin instead of a layer on top of it.

A dense base brush is useful when you want more coverage or need to buff product around the nose, hairline and jaw, where a sponge is awkward.

A fluffy blending brush is the workhorse for everything soft: eyeshadow transitions, blush, bronzer and diffusing hard edges. If you only buy one, make it this one.

The spoolie is the tool people overlook. It separates lashes, tames brow product that went on too heavy and blends out harsh lines. It costs almost nothing and saves a lot of faces.

What's a waste? Big sets with ten nearly identical brushes, novelty shapes you'll use once and anything marketed as a miracle tool. Expensive doesn't mean better either. A clean drugstore sponge beats a dirty luxury brush every time.

I learned this the hard way at an early bridal call in Brooklyn, when my brush roll stayed in a cab and I finished the whole face with a sponge, two borrowed brushes and my spoolie. The result was fine, and I stopped believing the size of a kit mattered.
