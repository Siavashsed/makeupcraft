## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does my foundation look orange or gray even though I matched my undertone?

As a working makeup artist, I hear this question every week. In most cases the undertone was not the problem. The real causes are usually depth and oxidation, and undertone gets blamed because 'warm or cool' is the only framework most people are ever taught.

Here is what usually happens.

1. The depth is slightly off. A foundation one step too deep reads orange or muddy on warmer skin and ashy on cooler skin. A shade that is too light can look gray or chalky. Your eye picks up a lightness mismatch long before it picks up a mismatch in undertone.

2. The formula oxidizes. Many long-wear foundations darken and warm up after they meet your skin's oils, often within 15 to 30 minutes. A shade that matched perfectly in the store can look orange by the time you get home. I once had a bride whose foundation had visibly shifted on her jaw just 20 minutes after I applied it.

3. The test method is unreliable. Vein color depends on lighting, skin thickness and circulation, which makes it a weak basis for picking an undertone. Swatching on your wrist or the back of your hand doesn't help either, because that skin is rarely the same color as your face.

What I recommend instead:
- Swatch three neighboring shades in stripes along your jawline.
- Wait at least 20 minutes, then check in daylight.
- Choose the stripe that disappears. If it darkened, go a shade lighter or try a formula that oxidizes less.
- Adjust undertone only after the depth is right.
