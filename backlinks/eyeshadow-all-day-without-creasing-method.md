## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does my eyeshadow still crease even when I use an eyeshadow primer?

I'm a professional makeup artist, and this is one of the most common questions I get. Here's the short answer: your primer usually isn't failing you. Your eyelid oil is winning.

Eyeshadow creases because two things break down whatever sits under the color: the natural oil your lids produce and the constant movement of blinking. Most people respond by adding more primer, but a thick layer makes the problem worse. It gives the oil more product to dissolve into, and it leaves a slick, flexible film that the lid fold pushes straight into a line.

What works is getting the lid under control before you pick up a single shade. My routine:

1. Thin primer. A rice-grain amount is enough for both eyes. Tap it in with a fingertip and give it a minute to set.

2. Powder lock. Press a translucent powder or a matte shadow close to your skin tone over the primer. This soaks up surface oil and gives the color something dry to grip.

3. Press, don't sweep. Pack the shadow on with a flat brush or your fingertip. Sweeping back and forth disturbs the base under it. Pressing sets the pigment into it. Blend the edges only after the lid is covered.

I learned this the hard way at a summer wedding in Palermo, where a heavy primer base gave out partway through the day. Since then, this approach has carried most of my clients' lids through 12 hours, heat included.

If you have very oily or hooded lids, blot the lid with a tissue before priming and touch up with powder only, never more primer.
