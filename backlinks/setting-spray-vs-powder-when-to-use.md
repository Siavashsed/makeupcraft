**Title:** Anyone else notice setting powder AND setting spray together can make mature or dry skin look worse, not better?

I do makeup professionally, and a mistake I made last spring taught me something I keep seeing in other people's routines. I was doing twelve faces at a bridal show in Lisbon, starting at 6:40 a.m. We were behind schedule, so I set everyone with the same loose powder. By nine o'clock the three brides with mature skin looked floured. Every fine line was highlighted, and I spent a long time fixing it.

Here's the rule of thumb I use now. Powder and spray do opposite jobs. Powder absorbs oil, so it suits skin that gets shiny. Spray adds a thin film that melts layers together and brings moisture back, so it suits dry skin or makeup that looks heavy and cakey. Layering both on the same area often cancels the benefit of each. You end up with a powdery layer that's been wetted down, and it can turn patchy.

What works for me is powder only where I actually get shiny (usually the T-zone), then spray over the whole face if it needs to look like skin again. Dry areas get spray only.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should I use setting spray or setting powder, and when should I use each?

They do opposite jobs, so the choice depends on your skin and on how your makeup looks once it's on.

Setting powder absorbs oil. If your skin gets shiny within a couple of hours, especially across the forehead, nose and chin, powder is the right tool. Press it on with a puff or a dense brush and focus on the areas that actually produce oil. You don't need to cover the whole face.

Setting spray works the other way. It deposits a very fine film that blends powder and foundation layers together, and it adds back a little moisture. That makes it the better choice if your skin is dry or mature, or if your makeup looks heavy, flat or cakey. A light mist can make a finished face look like skin again.

As a rough rule, powder for oil and spray for dryness or heaviness holds true about nine times out of ten.

The mistake I see most is layering both over the whole face. They tend to cancel each other out, and the result can look patchy. If you have combination skin, powder only the oily zones, then use spray over everything if it needs softening.

I learned this the hard way. At a bridal show in Lisbon last spring, I did twelve faces starting at 6:40 a.m. and set all of them with the same loose powder to save time. By nine o'clock the three brides with mature skin looked floured, and I had to spend a lot of time fixing it.
