**Title:** Does anyone else find eye shape charts put crease color in the wrong place for hooded lids?

Something I keep running into with hooded and deep-set eyes: the standard eye shape charts tell you to put your transition and crease shades on the natural crease. That works when the eye is closed. Once you open your eyes, the fold of skin covers most of it. The color either disappears or ends up as a muddy smudge right above the lash line.

What works better for me is skipping the almond/round/hooded labels and doing one check instead. Look straight ahead in the mirror with your eyes open and see how much mobile lid actually shows. If it's only a sliver, put the crease color above the fold, where you can still see it with your eyes open. Keep your liner thin so it doesn't eat up the little lid space you have. If plenty of lid shows, you can go thicker and place color lower.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Where should I put crease eyeshadow if I have hooded eyes?

Short answer: higher than you think. Put it just above the fold you can see with your eyes open, not on your actual crease.

I'm a makeup artist, and this is the most common mistake I see. Most eye shape charts put color in the natural crease. On hooded eyes, that crease sits under a fold of skin when your eyes are open. So you blend it beautifully, open your eyes, and the shade disappears or turns into a dark band near your lashes.

Here is how I approach it:

1. Check with your eyes open. Look straight into a mirror with your chin level and see how much of your mobile lid shows. Forget for a moment whether you are 'hooded' or 'almond.' What matters is how much lid is visible.

2. Place the crease color for the open eye. Tap a matte transition shade just above where the fold sits when your eyes are open. Blend up and out, and keep the deepest color toward the outer corner.

3. Check, then build. Open your eyes after each layer. If you can't see the color, move it up a little.

4. Size your liner to the visible lid. If only a sliver of lid shows, a thick liner will cover it. Keep the line thin and tight to the lashes, and only extend the wing as far as it stays visible with your eyes open.

5. Use mattes on the fold. Shimmer on the hood catches light and makes the fold stand out. Keep shine on the center of the visible lid.

The main takeaway: always judge your eye makeup with your eyes open, because that's how everyone else sees it.
