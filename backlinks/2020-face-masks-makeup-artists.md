**Title:** Did masks permanently change how you prioritize your face, even years later?

Something I keep coming back to when I think about 2020: masks didn't just change what people bought, they changed what makeup was for. With the lower half of the face covered most of the day, lipstick, contour and a lot of carefully built base work became invisible, or ended up smeared inside a mask.

My priorities flipped. Base went thinner and got set much harder around the nose and upper cheeks, where masks rub. Anything below the cheekbones went minimal. The eyes did all the talking: defined brows, more lash work, liner that actually reads above a mask, a bit of shimmer on the lid to catch light on video calls.

What I find interesting is how much of that stuck. A lot of people I know still wear a lighter base and a stronger eye than they did before 2020, with no mask in sight.

Did others notice the same shift? Which of your mask-era habits survived? Did you go back to lipstick, or never really return?

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How did wearing face masks in 2020 change the way makeup artists approach makeup?

As a working makeup artist and educator, I'd say it changed almost everything we teach. The biggest makeup story of 2020 wasn't a product launch. It was the mask.

Once public health guidance in the US told people to cover their faces in public, the lower half of the face stopped counting. That forced three big changes.

1. Base became about transfer, not coverage. A full-coverage foundation under a mask rubs off on the fabric, then the mask rubs what's left into patches. Artists moved to thinner layers, pressed in rather than buffed on top, and set the nose bridge, upper cheeks and chin much more firmly, because that's where the mask sits and moves.

2. Lip products lost their job. Lipstick ends up inside the mask and smeared across the chin. Most of us switched to lip balm or a light stain, or skipped the lips entirely unless the client planned to take the mask off for photos.

3. The eyes took over. With only the top third of the face showing, brows, lashes and liner had to do the work that a lip color or contour used to do. Liner got more deliberate, brows got more structure, and lid shimmer became a way to bring light to a face that was half covered.

I learned this the hard way at a courthouse wedding in Providence in late May 2020. I spent about forty minutes perfecting the bride's base, and then she put on a mask. That day changed how I plan every appointment.
