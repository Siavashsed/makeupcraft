## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are drugstore makeup products really as good as luxury brands, and which ones are worth buying?

Some of them are, and in specific categories a few are actually better. I'm a professional makeup artist, and I've stopped assuming that a higher price means higher performance.

Here's the moment that settled it for me. Last March I was doing a 6 a.m. bridal call in a humid Savannah hotel room. I'd trusted a $34 setting spray for years. By hour four it had let go of the bride's T-zone. The $9 NYX bottle sitting in my bag held until the last photo. I haven't reached for the expensive one on a humid day since.

That said, it isn't true across the board. Here's how I think about it.

Where drugstore often wins: setting sprays, mascaras, brow products, lip liners, and many powders. These categories depend on well-understood technology like film formers, polymers, and pigment suspension, and mass-market brands have refined them extensively. You're rarely paying for a better formula in these categories, mostly for packaging and marketing.

Where luxury can still justify the cost: certain skincare actives, some complexion products with unusually refined textures, and fragrance. Here the ingredient quality and the sensory experience can genuinely differ.

How to judge any product yourself:
1. Test it in the conditions you'll actually wear it, whether that's heat, humidity, or a long day.
2. Check how it looks at hour six, not minute five.
3. Look at the ingredient list rather than the price tag.
4. Compare it directly against what you already own.
