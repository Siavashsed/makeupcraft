I'm a makeup artist, and last March I set up a rolling rack of 42 reds in my studio to test on a client with warm olive skin and slightly purple lips. Almost every one that looked gorgeous in the tube went wrong once it was on her mouth.

Here's what I took from it. Most advice stops at undertone, but undertone only gets you the hue family. You also need to think about depth, meaning how much intensity your skin can carry before the lipstick looks like it's wearing you. Then there's your natural lip color, which shows through the formula and shifts the final shade. On my client, purple-toned lips pulled a lot of otherwise flattering reds toward a cold, muddy berry.

My fix is to test on the lips, with a thin layer first, and in daylight if you can. Blot, then judge it after a minute, once the shade has settled.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I choose the right red lipstick for my skin undertone?

I'm a professional makeup artist, and this is the question I get asked most. The short answer is that undertone matters, but it only covers one of three things you need to check.

First, undertone decides hue. If your skin leans warm (golden, peachy, olive), orange-based and brick reds usually look natural on you. If it leans cool (pink or blue-ish), blue-based reds and cherry shades tend to flatter. If you're neutral, you have more room to move in both directions.

Second, depth decides intensity. A very deep, saturated red can overwhelm fair skin, and a sheer, soft red can disappear on deeper skin. Match how much color your complexion can carry, not just its temperature.

Third, and most people skip this, your natural lip color changes the final shade. Lipstick is slightly translucent, so what's underneath shows through. Last March I tested 42 reds on a client with warm olive skin and slightly purple lips. Many that looked perfect in the tube turned cooler and darker on her. A thin base of concealer or a neutral lip liner can fix that.

One practical tip: don't swatch on your wrist or the back of your hand. That skin is a different color from your lips and gives you misleading results. Test on your actual lips instead, apply a thin layer, blot, and give it a minute to settle. Check it in daylight if you can, because store lighting is rarely honest.
